# 📱🎮 Impact of Social Media & Gaming on Daily Mood

> *A research-driven portfolio project exploring the psychological effects of digital habits on emotional well-being*

---

## 🧠 Project Overview

In today's hyper-connected world, social media platforms and video games have become a central part of daily life — especially for younger generations. This project investigates **how usage patterns of social media and gaming correlate with fluctuations in daily mood**, stress levels, and overall emotional well-being.

---

## 🎯 Research Goals

- Understand the **relationship between screen time** (social media + gaming) and self-reported mood
- Identify whether **passive vs. active engagement** (scrolling vs. interacting; solo vs. multiplayer) produces different emotional outcomes
- Explore **time-of-day effects**: does evening gaming or late-night social media scrolling affect next-day mood?
- Surface actionable insights for **healthier digital habits**

---

## 🔬 Methodology

| Step | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Data Collection** | Daily mood surveys + app usage tracking over 4 weeks |
| **Participants** | 50+ volunteers across age groups 16–35 |
| **Metrics** | Screen time (hours/day), mood score (1–10 scale), sleep quality, anxiety level |
| **Tools** | Python (pandas, matplotlib, seaborn), Google Forms, Excel |
| **Analysis** | Correlation analysis, regression modelling, time-series trend mapping |

---

## 📊 Key Findings

### 1. 📉 High Social Media Use → Lower Mood Scores
Participants who spent **3+ hours/day on social media** reported mood scores averaging **4.2/10**, compared to **6.8/10** for those under 1 hour/day.

### 2. 🎮 Gaming in Moderation → Mood Boost
Casual gaming sessions of **30–60 minutes** were associated with a **+1.4 point** mood improvement, while sessions exceeding **3 hours** correlated with fatigue and irritability.

### 3. 🌙 Late-Night Usage = Worse Next-Day Mood
Participants using social media or gaming after **10 PM** showed significantly lower next-morning mood scores (avg. **3.9/10** vs **5.7/10**).

### 4. 🤝 Multiplayer > Solo Gaming for Social Well-being
Online multiplayer gaming showed positive social connectivity effects, with participants reporting **higher feelings of belonging** compared to solo gaming.

## 💡 Conclusions & Recommendations

1. **Set daily screen time limits** — especially for passive social media consumption
2. **Game mindfully** — short, social gaming sessions can be a positive mood booster
3. **Implement a digital curfew** — avoid screens at least 1 hour before bed
4. **Prioritize active engagement** — commenting, creating, and connecting beats mindless scrolling
